\noindent
A few notes about the sets defined in this problem:\\
\\
The set \(T\) of years contains only \(\{0,1\}\) which map to \(5\) and \(10\) years respectively. Defining the set this way simplifies the logic when it comes to program in Python/Gurobi. \\
\\
The set \(U\) of supplier capacity upgrades has the values \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) which represent no upgrade (0), and the upgrade options 1, 2, and 3. \\
\\
The set \(\Omega\) defines the different scenarios that can take place over the 5-10 year period from now. The possible scenarios are demand is down by (-20\%), as forecasted (0\%), or up by (+20\%)

\subsection{Objective}
\bigskip
The objective should be to minimise the upgrade cost (but of course, we still need to meet the demand constraints with growing demand).\\
\\
Communication 6
\[\min{\left\{\sum_{n\in N}\left[\sum_{u \in U}c_{n,u} z_{n,u}\right]\right\}}\]
\smallskip\noindent
Communication 7
\[\min{\left\{\sum_{n\in N}\sum_{u \in U}c_{n,u} z_{n,u} + k \sum_{p \in P}\psi_p l_p\right\}}\]
\smallskip\noindent
Communication 8
\smallskip\noindent
\[\min{\left\{\sum_{t\in T}\left[\sum_{n\in S}\sum_{u \in U}c_{n,u}  (1 - \delta_{t}) z_{n,t,u} + k \sum_{p \in P}(1 - \delta_{t})\psi_{p,t} l_p\right]\right\}}\]
\smallskip\noindent
Communications 9 and 10

\begin{align}
    &\min{\left\{\E_{\omega\in \Omega}\left[\sum_{t\in T}\left[\sum_{n\in S}\sum_{u \in U}c_{n,u}  (1 - \delta_{t}) z_{n,t,u,\omega} + k \sum_{p \in P}(1 - \delta_{t})\psi_{p,t,\omega} l_p\right]\right]\right\}} \\
    =& \min{\left\{\sum_{\omega \in \Omega} \theta_\omega \cdot\left[\sum_{t\in T}\left[\sum_{n\in S}\sum_{u \in U}c_{n,u}  (1 - \delta_{t}) z_{n,t,u,\omega} + k \sum_{p \in P}(1 - \delta_{t})\psi_{p,t,\omega} l_p\right]\right] \right\}} 
\end{align}

\bigskip
Where:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \(\theta_\omega = \frac{1}{3}, \hspace{1cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\)
    \item \(k = \$ 200,000\)
    \item \(\delta_t = \begin{cases}
    0.0, & \text{if } t = 0 \\
    0.3, & \text{if } t = 1
    \end{cases}\)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Constraints}

\noindent
\subsubsection{Supplier Capacity}
%\[x_{n,t}\leq a_{n,0} + \sum_{\substack{u \in U \\ u \neq 0}}\left\{a_{n,u}z_{n,u}\right\}, \hspace{1cm} \forall n\in N \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T\]
% ^^ for previous to comm 8 (not including)
\[x_{n,t,\omega}\leq a_{n,0} + \sum_{\substack{u \in U \\ u \neq 0}}\left\{a_{n,u} \sum_{\substack{t^*\in T \\ t^* \leq t}}z_{n,t^*,u,\omega}\right\}, \hspace{1cm} \forall n\in N \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]
\smallskip\noindent
Where \(a_{n,0}\) denotes the base capacity (with no upgrades) for node \(n \in N\). Of course only \(a_{n,0} \geq 0\) for \(n \in S\).\\
\\
We also have

\[x_{n,t,\omega} = 0, \hspace{1cm} \forall n\in N \setminus S \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]
... as we cannot produce gas at a normal node.

\bigskip
\subsubsection{Supplier Upgrades}
We are constrained to choosing either one of the upgrades per supplier node or no upgrade at all. Additionally, any given supplier node can have at most one upgrade over the 10 year period.
\[\sum_{\substack{u\in U \\ u \neq 0}}\sum_{t\in T} z_{n,t,u,\omega} \leq 1, \hspace{0.5cm} \forall n \in S \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]
\noindent
For any given generator and for any given year, exactly 1 level must be chosen (including no upgrade)
\[\sum_{u\in U} z_{n,t,u,\omega} = 1, \hspace{0.5cm} \forall n \in S \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]
\noindent
For any given node that is not a generator, no upgrades can be selected.
\[\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{t\in T} z_{n,t,u,\omega} = 0, \hspace{0.5cm} \forall n \in N \setminus S \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]

\bigskip
\noindent
\subsubsection{Pipeline Capacity}
The pipelines have a fixed capacity, however, these can be upgraded to double their existing capacity at a cost. We have a binary variable to decide whether to upgrade the pipe at time \(t \in T\) which takes the values 0 or 1. Then the maximum flow of gas in the pipe is bounded by a function of the binary variable multiplied by the pipe's base capacity.

\[Y_{p,t,\omega} \leq \left(1 + \sum_{\substack{t^*\in T \\ t^* \leq t}}\psi_{p,t^*,\omega}\right) b, \hspace{1cm} \forall p \in P \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]
\\
Where \(b = 281 \text{MJ}\).
\smallskip
\noindent 
We also need to make the constraint that any given pipe \(p \in P\) can only be upgraded at most once.

\[\sum_{t \in T} \psi_{p,t,\omega} \leq 1, \hspace{1cm} \forall p \in P \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]


\newpage
\noindent
\subsubsection{Scenario Constraints}
Given that the problem says we cannot know which scenario we will experience in time step 1, all the upgrades in time step 0 must be the same. We can do this by constraining the upgrade variables to be equal to each other.

\begin{align}
    z_{n,0,u,0} &= z_{n,0,u,1}, & \hspace{1cm} \forall n \in N \hspace{0.5cm} \forall u \in U \\
    z_{n,0,u,1} &= z_{n,0,u,2}, & \hspace{1cm} \forall n \in N \hspace{0.5cm} \forall u \in U \\
    \psi_{p,0,0} &= \psi_{p,0,1}, & \hspace{1cm} \forall p \in P \\
    \psi_{p,0,1} &= \psi_{p,0,2}, & \hspace{1cm} \forall p \in P
\end{align}

\bigskip
\noindent
\subsubsection{Flow Balance Constraints}
We must create a flow balance constraint to say that the gas produced plus the inflow of gas must equal the demand of gas plus the outflow of gas at each node on the grid. Mathematically, this is very similar to the first assignment and can be written as

\[x_{n,t,\omega}+\sum_{\substack{p\in P \\ \tau_p = n}}y_{p,t,\omega} = d_{n,t} (1 + \lambda_{t,\omega}) + \sum_{\substack{p\in P \\ f_p = n}}y_{p,t,\omega}, \hspace{1cm} \forall n \in N \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]
\smallskip\noindent
Where
\(\boldsymbol{\lambda} = 
    \begin{bmatrix}
    0.0 & 0.0 & 0.0 \\
    -0.2 & 0.0 & 0.2
    \end{bmatrix}\)

\bigskip
\subsubsection{Communication 10 Upgrade Constraint}
Communication 10 states \textit{"the undiscounted upgrade costs in the second 5-year period should never be more than twice the upgrade costs in the first 5-year period."}\\
\\
We can very easily translate this into a mathematical constraint as follows

\[{SU}_{1} + {PU}_{1} \leq 2 \left[{SU}_{0} + {PU}_{0}\right], \hspace{1cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]

\noindent
Where

\begin{align}
    {SU}_{t} &= \sum_{n \in S}\sum_{u \in U} c_{n,u} z_{n,t,u,\omega} \\
    {PU}_{t} &= k \sum_{p \in P}  \psi_{p,t,\omega} l_{p}
\end{align}

\noindent
for \(t \in T\). Again, where \(k = \$ 200,000\).

\noindent

\bigskip

\subsubsection{Non-negativity}
\bigskip
Non-negativity constraints are (fortunately) given to us when using Gurobi, however, it is still important that they are defined in the mathematical model for understanding and completeness.\\
\\
We have

\[x_{n,t,\omega} \geq 0, \hspace{1cm} \forall n \in N \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega\]
... as gas-producing nodes (gas stations) produce a non-negative amount of gas.
We also have

\begin{align*}
    y_{p,t,\omega} \geq 0,& \hspace{1cm} \forall p \in P \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega \\
    z_{n,t,u,\omega} \in \{0,1\},& \hspace{1cm} \forall n \in N \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T \hspace{0.5cm} \forall u \in U \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega \\
    \psi_{p,t,\omega} \in \{0,1\},& \hspace{1cm} \forall p \in P \hspace{0.5cm} \forall t \in T \hspace{0.5cm} \forall \omega \in \Omega
\end{align*}

\bigskip

\subsection{Insights Into The Solution}
\bigskip
One of the key constraints which limited the outcome of the model was the maximum pipeline capacity value of 281 MJ. If this value were to increase it would further reduce the optimal total upgrade cost, as there would be less demand for the pipelines to be upgraded. Since the current network would have a greater capacity to distribute gas from the supplier nodes to all of the other nodes in order to meet the demand. Likewise, if the initial capacities of each of the supplier nodes were increased, it would also reduce the total optimal cost, since there would be less of a demand for supplier nodes to undergo upgrades. 
\bigskip
\\
Furthermore, if the costs associated with upgrading the pipelines or upgrade costs for the supplier nodes were to be reduced, this would intuitively reduce the total optimal cost to meet forecasted demands. Similarly, if the discount factor for the second year was increased higher than 30\% the corresponding optimal cost would also be reduced.
\bigskip
\\
Another key constraint is the forecasted demand of each node for both time periods. If in the real world situation, the demand was higher or lower than the forecasted value this would have an impact on which upgrades need to be conducted and in what time period. For example, if the forecasted demand was greater than the realised demand, then Pacific Paradise Gas may have unnecessarily upgraded the supplier nodes and pipelines. Vice versa that if the forecasted demand was lower than the realised demand, they may not have upgraded the pipelines and supplier nodes when it was necessary, and hence may not be able to meet their demand. 
\bigskip
\\
If the likelihoods for each of the three scenarios in the second 5-year period were to change, this would subsequently change the optimal expected upgrade cost determined by the model. For example, if it was more likely for Scenario 3 (the upturn, in which the realised demand is 20\% higher) to occur, then it would mean that the overall expected cost would increase as there would be a higher likelihood of more upgrades needing to be undergone in order to meet the demands of the upturn scenario.
\bigskip
\\
The optimal cost has been constrained by the ability for each supplier node to only be upgraded once. Although this is feasible for the two time periods for this assignment, it may be necessary for Pacific Paradise Gas to allow the Supplier Nodes to be upgraded multiple times if the demand continues to increase at such a large rate. 
\bigskip
\\
Finally, Pacific Paradise Gas has not indicated the time duration that it would take for the supplier node upgrades to be completed. It is also unknown if there would be an impact on producing gas from the supplier nodes for the duration of their upgrade. This would have a major impact on the supply of the gas, and the upgrades may need to occur at varying times over the 10 years.
